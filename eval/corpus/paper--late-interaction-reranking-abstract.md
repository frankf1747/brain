# Late-interaction reranking for long documents

Abstract. Cross-encoder rerankers are the most accurate second stage for retrieval pipelines, but their cost grows quickly with document length, and most systems truncate long documents to the first few hundred tokens before scoring them, discarding exactly the passages that often hold the answer. We present SpanRank, a late-interaction reranker that scores a document by its best-matching spans rather than by a single pass over truncated text. At indexing time SpanRank stores contextual token embeddings for every document; at query time it compares the query's token embeddings against sliding spans of each candidate and aggregates the strongest span scores into a document score, so no document text is re-encoded per query. Across 12 datasets the method cut the top-20 retrieval failure rate by 21 percent relative to a truncating cross-encoder, and added 38 milliseconds of latency per query on a single GPU. Gains were largest for documents longer than 4,000 tokens, where truncation discards the most text, and smallest on short passage collections, where both rerankers see the whole document. We release code, index-building scripts and per-dataset results, and discuss the storage cost of keeping token embeddings, which we reduce by a factor of four with product quantization at a small loss in accuracy.
